# Drones for Teens (Ages 12-15) — Getting Started

How a 12-15 year old can get into building and flying drones — without burning out after the first crash. Written for both the teen and the parent.

> **For the teen:** This isn't about toy drones. It's the real hobby — the same gear racing pilots and cinematographers use, just sized to a budget and a learning curve. Stick with it past month one and you'll be repairing electronics, soldering circuit boards, programming flight controllers, and flying as well as adults twice your age.
>
> **For the parent:** This is a serious STEM hobby — electronics, soldering, basic programming, physics, regulations, video editing. Costs scale with commitment. Read the [Parents section](#parents--guardians-guide) first.

## The Honest Pitch

**You will crash.** A lot. That's the whole hobby in a sentence. Pilots who quit are the ones who expected to fly clean on day one. Pilots who stay are the ones who learned that a snapped prop is a 30-second fix, not a tragedy.

Everything in this guide is designed around that fact:

- Cheap first drones that bounce off walls instead of breaking
- A simulator stage so your first real flight isn't your first flight ever
- A repair stage so your first crash teaches you something instead of ending things
- Real community at the end so you have people to fly with

You don't need talent. You need patience and ~$150-300 of starter gear.

---

## The 5-Stage Path

```
Stage 0  →  Stage 1  →  Stage 2  →  Stage 3  →  Stage 4  →  Stage 5
 sim       indoor      outdoor     repair      first       community
(free)     whoop       micro       + tune      real        + racing
           ($100)      ($200)                  build
                                               ($500)
```

Each stage takes weeks to months. **Don't skip stages.** Kids who jump straight to a 5" build crash it within an hour and lose interest. Kids who do the simulator + whoop path first build skills that transfer.

## Stage 0 — Try the Simulator (Free / Cheap)

**Goal:** Confirm you actually like this before spending real money.

| What | Cost | Notes |
|------|-----:|-------|
| **DRL Simulator** (Steam) | Free | Official Drone Racing League sim — solid free entry |
| **FPV Freerider** | ~$5 | Cheapest paid sim, runs on almost any laptop |
| **Velocidrone** | $20 | The "real" sim — what serious pilots actually use |
| **Liftoff** | $20 | Best graphics, good freestyle feel |
| **Game controller** | $25-40 | Any USB game controller works for a start (Xbox/PS pad, etc.) |

**Day-one plan:**
1. Download DRL Simulator (free) on a laptop
2. Plug in any game controller
3. Try to hover for 10 minutes
4. Try to fly through a gate
5. If you're still having fun after an hour → continue to Stage 1

**You'll fly badly. That's the point.** Stick with it 5-10 hours over a week or two. By the end you'll be able to hover, turn, and land — and that's enough to graduate to a real drone.

> **Tip:** When you do buy a real radio in Stage 2, plug it into your PC via USB — sims see it as a controller. Your sim time keeps counting and your muscle memory transfers exactly.

See [VIDEOS.md Section 2 — Simulator](./VIDEOS.md#2-simulator-practice-buy-radio-first-fly-this) for tutorial videos.

## Stage 1 — Your First Drone (Indoor Whoop)

**Goal:** Fly a real drone indoors safely. Build flight reflexes the sim can't teach.

A **Tiny Whoop** is a 65-75mm ducted micro quadcopter (~25-35g). It bounces off walls, can't hurt people or pets, won't break furniture, and replacement parts cost $3-5. It's the most teen-friendly drone in existence.

**Two paths:**

### Path A — Ready-to-Fly Kit (everything included)

If you have nothing yet and want one-box simplicity:

| Kit | Price | Includes | Why |
|-----|-----:|----------|-----|
| **BetaFPV Cetus Pro RTF** | $120-150 | Drone + radio + goggles | Most popular teen starter; built-in altitude hold to make first flights forgiving |
| **BetaFPV Cetus X HD** | $250-300 | Drone + radio + HD goggles | Bigger jump in price but HD video; harder to justify as a first kit |

**Honest note:** the cheap radio and goggles in the RTF kits are throwaway — they won't grow with you. You'll replace both within 6-12 months. Good for a "do I like this?" test, not a long-term investment.

### Path B — Build the Real Setup Now (Recommended)

If you're confident this is the hobby, skip RTF and buy gear you'll keep:

| Item | Price | Notes |
|------|-----:|-------|
| **RadioMaster Pocket (ELRS)** | $65-80 | Real radio — you'll use this for everything, even your 5" build later |
| **BetaFPV Meteor75 (ELRS) BNF** | $90-110 | Best beginner whoop, durable, great parts support |
| **Eachine EV800D goggles** | $50-70 | Cheap analog box goggles for whoop |
| **6× 1S 450mAh batteries + USB charger** | $30-40 | Enough for ~20 minutes of total fly time per charging session |
| **Total** | **$235-300** | Nothing wasted — everything carries forward to bigger builds |

**This is the path most experienced pilots wish they had taken.** You spend a bit more up front but never re-buy.

See [BUILD-GUIDE.md — Tiny Whoop section](./BUILD-GUIDE.md#optional-indoor-training-drone-tiny-whoop) for the same info from the adult-build perspective.

### What to actually do with the whoop

- **Week 1:** Hover. Just hover. In your bedroom, eyes on the screen of the goggles. 5-minute sessions, lots of crashes, no rush.
- **Week 2:** Slow turns. Forward flight. Fly through a doorway.
- **Week 3:** Multiple rooms. Around chair legs. Slow figure-eights.
- **Week 4+:** Add gates (cheap cardboard arches). Try a small acro flip. Race a friend.

A whoop session = 5-10 short flights of 2-3 minutes each. You'll fly more in one afternoon than the sim teaches in a week.

## Stage 2 — Real Radio + Outdoor Micro

**Goal:** Move outside on your family land. Real freedom, bigger drone, longer flights.

**Since you have private land, this stage opens up early.** You don't have to wait for a club or worry about flying near strangers — your backyard is your training field.

### Outdoor micros that bridge whoop → 5"

These are 75mm-100mm quads with more power than indoor whoops, designed for outdoor flight in light wind:

| Drone | Size | Price | Notes |
|-------|-----:|------:|-------|
| **BetaFPV Pavo20 (DJI O3 / O4 Lite)** | 2" | $200-260 | Light outdoor cinewhoop, HD goggles compatible if you have DJI |
| **Happymodel Mobula7 1S/2S** | 75mm | $80-100 | Whoop-sized but flies outdoors in calm conditions |
| **BetaFPV Meteor85 (analog)** | 85mm | $90-110 | Brushless outdoor micro, more power than the Meteor75 |
| **Emax TinyHawk III** | 75mm | $80-100 | Tough, intended for indoor AND outdoor |

**Recommended:** If you already bought the Meteor75 from Stage 1, fly it outdoors on calm days first. When you outgrow it, the Meteor85 or a 3" cinewhoop is the natural next step.

### What changes outdoors

- **Real wind matters** — anything over 15 km/h will push a micro around
- **Range becomes real** — your radio link can fail at distance, so set a failsafe (in BetaFlight: motor stop on signal loss)
- **GPS Rescue helps** — add a small GPS module ($12-15) and configure return-to-home in BetaFlight 2026.6+ (GPS Rescue was rebuilt in 2026.6 and is meaningfully more precise than older versions)
- **Batteries die faster** — outdoor flight uses more power; carry 6+ packs

### Critical rule for your private land

Even on your own property, you must:

- **Stay under 120m AGL** (legal limit in EU Open category)
- **Check [droneregler.dk/dronezoner](https://www.droneregler.dk/dronezoner)** — your land might fall under an airport's controlled airspace regardless of who owns it
- **Stay in visual line-of-sight (VLOS)** — don't fly over the property line where you can't see
- **Be registered** (parent registers on your behalf if under 15 — see [Parents section](#parents--guardians-guide))

## Stage 3 — First Repair, First Tuning

**Goal:** When you crash and break something, fix it yourself. This is the moment you stop being a "drone owner" and start being a "drone pilot."

Most kids who quit do so at the first real break — broken motor, snapped frame arm, ripped wire. The kids who stay learn:

- **How to swap a motor** (4 screws, plug in 3 wires)
- **How to replace a prop** (one screw)
- **How to charge LiPo safely** ([LiPo safety video — must watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3urBpFIBgY))
- **How to flash and configure BetaFlight** (free software, plug in via USB-C)

This stage takes 2-3 months of casual repair and tinkering. By the end you'll know:

- What every wire on the flight controller does
- How to bind your radio to a receiver
- How to set up flight modes (angle, horizon, acro)
- How to read battery voltage and know when to land

### Tools you'll need (~$80 total)

| Tool | Price | Why |
|------|-----:|-----|
| **Pinecil V2 soldering iron** | $30 | Tiny, USB-C powered, perfect entry iron |
| **Solder wire (60/40 leaded, 0.8mm)** | $8 | Easier to learn than lead-free |
| **Flux paste** | $5 | Makes soldering 10× easier |
| **Hex drivers (1.5mm, 2.0mm, 2.5mm)** | $10 | All FPV frame screws |
| **Side cutters + tweezers** | $15 | Trim wires, hold parts |
| **LiPo charging bag** | $10 | Fire safety, mandatory |

> **Soldering must be supervised by an adult until you're 14-15.** The iron is 350°C — that's a serious burn. Set up a dedicated table, use a stand, and never leave it powered on unattended.

See [SOLDERING-GUIDE.md](./SOLDERING-GUIDE.md) for the full setup and technique guide.

## Stage 4 — First Real Build (with Adult Help)

**Goal:** Build a full 5" quadcopter from individual parts. Real soldering, real wiring, real firmware setup.

This is the big one. The 5" build is what every adult guide in this repo is about — and a 13-15 year old absolutely can do it, with adult supervision on the soldering iron.

### When you're ready

You're ready when you can:
- ✅ Fly a whoop confidently in acro mode (no auto-leveling)
- ✅ Solder a motor wire to an ESC pad without help
- ✅ Explain what a flight controller does
- ✅ Read a wiring diagram

That's typically **6-12 months** after starting the simulator.

### What to build

**Don't build premium first.** Use the [Budget Build from BUDGET-TIERS.md](./BUDGET-TIERS.md#budget-build-500-630-all-in) — ~$500 all-in including radio, goggles, and tools (or less if you already have the radio and tools from Stages 1-3).

| What | Price (incremental) | Notes |
|------|-------------------:|-------|
| Frame, FC, ESC, motors, props, VTX, camera | $200-280 | The actual quad |
| Receiver + antenna | $25-35 | ELRS, matches your existing radio |
| Batteries (3× 1300mAh 4S) | $60-75 | First three packs |
| (Skip tools if you have them from Stage 3) | -$80 | |
| **Incremental cost if you already have radio, goggles, soldering kit** | **~$285-390** | |

This is the realistic "first real build" cost when your earlier stages have funded the supporting gear.

### The build itself

Follow [BUILD-GUIDE.md](./BUILD-GUIDE.md). The 11-step build order works for any age — kid or adult. The difference is:

- **An adult should supervise the soldering steps** (steps 2, 4, 5, 6 of the build order)
- **An adult should be present for first power-on with a smoke stopper** (a $5 inline fuse that prevents fires from short circuits)
- **An adult should do the first arm/disarm test with props off**

## Stage 5 — Community, Racing, Creating

**Goal:** Find your people. This is where the hobby becomes a passion.

Flying alone in the backyard is fun. Flying with other people who get it is life-changing.

### Find a club

- **[Modelflyvning Danmark](https://modelflyvning.dk)** — the Danish national model-flying association. They have youth memberships and clubs in most regions. Insured legal flying, mentors, and other kids/teens.
- **Local FPV racing chapters** — search "FPV racing [your city]" on Facebook or Discord. Many regions have informal weekly meetups.

### Try drone racing

- **[MultiGP](https://www.multigp.com/)** — the global drone racing league. Has youth/junior classes for under-16.
- **Local race events** — many model clubs run informal races; great low-pressure way to meet people.

### Create content

Many of the top FPV pilots and YouTubers started as teens making videos in their backyards. The DVR recording from your goggles is a complete video file — just trim it and upload.

- Free editing software: **DaVinci Resolve** (Mac/Windows, professional-grade, free)
- Easy mobile editing: **CapCut** (free, mobile)
- Channel inspiration: BotGrinder, Mr Steele, Le Drib — all started small

See [VIDEOS.md Section 8 — Inspiration](./VIDEOS.md#8-inspiration--what-youre-working-toward) for what's possible.

## Safety — Non-Negotiable

**Read this even if you skip everything else.**

### LiPo battery safety

LiPo (lithium polymer) batteries can catch fire if mishandled. Real fires, real damage to your house. This is the most important safety rule in the hobby.

- ✅ **Always charge inside a LiPo-safe bag.** Never on furniture or carpet.
- ✅ **Never leave charging unattended.** Stay in the room.
- ✅ **Store at storage voltage (3.8V/cell).** Never store fully charged for more than a day or two.
- ✅ **A puffed/swollen pack is trash.** Dispose at a battery recycling center, never throw in regular trash.
- ✅ **Never pierce or crush a LiPo.** If you crash and a pack looks damaged, put it in a metal bucket outside for a few hours before disposing.
- ❌ **Never charge in a car, in direct sun, or anywhere flammable.**

**Watch this BEFORE charging your first pack:** [How to keep LiPos from burning down your house](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3urBpFIBgY) — Joshua Bardwell

### Soldering iron safety

- The tip is 350°C — instant serious burn
- Always use a stand; never lay it down on the table
- Solder fumes are toxic — open a window, use a small fan
- Eye protection (cheap safety glasses) when clipping wires — they fly
- Wash hands after every session (solder contains lead)
- **Under 14: adult must be at the table while you solder.** Not just in the house — at the table.
- **14-15: adult must check your setup before you plug in.** Soldering solo is fine once trusted.

### Propeller safety

A 5" propeller spinning at 30,000+ RPM will cut fingers. Not "scrape." Cut.

- ❌ **NEVER mount props until you're ready to fly outside.** Bench testing, configuration, repairs — always props OFF.
- ✅ **First arm of the day: face the quad away from you and any people.**
- ✅ **If a motor twitches unexpectedly while plugged in, immediately unplug the battery — DO NOT touch the quad.**

### Flying safety

- Set a failsafe in BetaFlight (motor stop on signal loss) — test it on the bench BEFORE flying
- Set a battery voltage alarm (3.5V/cell minimum)
- Carry a fire extinguisher if you fly in dry grass conditions
- Stay 30m from any people who haven't agreed to be near a drone

## How to Keep Interest (Avoid Burning Out)

The #1 reason teens quit: **they spend too much on day one, crash it on day three, and feel terrible about wasting their parents' money.**

The path in this guide is designed to prevent that. Some additional retention rules:

### 1. Celebrate the first crash.
Not survive it — *celebrate*. Take a photo of the broken prop. Replace it. Move on. The pilots who quit are the ones who experience their first crash as a disaster. The pilots who stay see it as the first repair they get to do themselves.

### 2. Fly every week, even if just 10 minutes.
Consistency beats intensity. Twenty minutes a week for a year teaches you more than ten hours one weekend then nothing.

### 3. Mix indoor and outdoor.
Bad weather day? Whoop indoors. Sunny calm day? Outdoor micro. Always have something flyable so you never lose momentum.

### 4. Find one person to fly with.
A sibling, a friend, an online Discord buddy. Anyone. Flying alone gets old; flying with someone is forever.

### 5. Document your progress.
A simple folder of crash videos, repair photos, and flight clips. Watching yourself improve over months is the strongest motivation in the hobby.

### 6. Don't buy the next thing until you've mastered this thing.
Resist the urge to upgrade until your current quad bores you. Every upgrade resets your skill — you'll fly the new one worse than the old one for a few weeks.

### 7. Set one small goal at a time.
Not "become a great pilot." Try "fly through this doorway 5 times in a row." Then "do a flip." Then "land on this mat." Small wins compound.

### 8. When stuck, ask for help.
The FPV community is unusually welcoming to beginners. ArduPilot/PX4/FPV Discord servers, RCGroups forums, Reddit's r/fpv — ask any question, you'll get a real answer.

## Parents / Guardians Guide

A short section for parents, written plainly.

### What this hobby actually teaches

Real STEM skills, not toy-level:

- **Electronics** — voltage, current, power, soldering, troubleshooting circuits
- **Programming** — flight controller firmware (BetaFlight, iNav) is configured via a real desktop app; iNav waypoint missions are basic programming
- **Physics** — lift, drag, thrust, battery chemistry, RF propagation
- **Mechanical engineering** — frame design, motor mounting, vibration damping
- **Video editing** — every flight produces footage to edit
- **Regulations** — drone law is real aviation law; learning it is genuinely educational
- **Project management** — building a drone is a 20-step project requiring planning, parts sourcing, and patience

### Cost reality

| Stage | Cumulative spend | Time investment |
|-------|----------------:|----------------|
| Stage 0 (sim) | $0-25 | 1-2 weeks |
| Stage 1 (whoop kit) | $120-300 | 1-3 months |
| Stage 2 (outdoor micro) | $200-350 (or already covered) | 2-6 months |
| Stage 3 (repair tools) | +$80 | 2-4 months |
| Stage 4 (first build) | +$300-400 incremental | 4-12 months |
| Stage 5 (clubs, racing) | $50-200/year | ongoing |

Realistic total to a complete kit and first real build: **$700-1000 over 12-18 months.** Most teens never get to Stage 4. That's fine — Stages 1-3 are a complete hobby on their own.

### The Danish legal requirement for under-15s

**Important:** Under Danish law (Trafikstyrelsen / droneregler.dk), a child under 15 cannot be the registered drone operator. **You, as the parent, must register on the child's behalf** using your MitID/NemID at [droneregler.dk](https://www.droneregler.dk/fritidsdroneflyvning/registrer-dig-som-privat-droneoperatoer).

Source (direct quote from droneregler.dk):
> *"Hvis du ansøger som privat person eller på vegne af et barn (under 15 år), skal du anvende dette link"* — *"If you apply as a private person OR ON BEHALF OF A CHILD (under 15), use this link."*

At 15 the child can register themselves with their own MitID. Until then, you are the legal operator and are liable for damages.

### What this means practically

- The drone has YOUR operator number on it
- Liability falls on YOU if anything goes wrong
- Insurance is mandatory — check your household *ansvarsforsikring* covers drone flying; if not, separate drone insurance is ~€50-150/year
- **You have private land**, which simplifies most of this — the child can fly without worrying about A3's 150m-from-residential rule, as long as the land itself is big enough that no uninvolved person is within range
- Still must stay under 120m AGL and check [droneregler.dk/dronezoner](https://www.droneregler.dk/dronezoner) — your land could fall in restricted airspace regardless of ownership

See [REGULATIONS.md](./REGULATIONS.md) for the full legal picture.

### How to support without taking over

This hobby goes wrong when parents either:
- (A) Buy everything and do it for them — kid never learns
- (B) Refuse to be involved — kid burns out alone

The sweet spot is:
- **Be there for soldering and first power-ups** (safety)
- **Drive them to the field** (logistics)
- **Watch their crash videos with them** (interest)
- **Let them research and choose their own parts** (autonomy)
- **Let them spend their own money where possible** (commitment)
- **Don't bail them out of every problem** — being stuck is where learning happens

### When to say no

| Situation | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "I want a $1000 quad on day one" | No. Stage 0 first. |
| "I want to fly without you supervising the iron" | Not until you're 14-15 and have practiced 10+ joints supervised |
| "I want to fly over the neighbor's house" | Hard no. Privacy law in Denmark is strict. |
| "I want to fly at the local park" | Only if it's clear of people and not in a drone no-fly zone (check map) |
| "I want to add a 4k camera and fly higher than 120m" | No. 120m is the legal ceiling regardless. |

## Stuff to Avoid

Some products marketed at kids that will set them back, not forward:

| Avoid | Why | Buy instead |
|-------|-----|-------------|
| **Toy quads from Amazon ($20-50)** | No skill transfer; built-in stabilization makes them feel like cheating; they fall apart | A real whoop (Stage 1) |
| **Drones with "auto-takeoff/auto-landing/follow me" features** | Camera drones, not FPV; teaches nothing about flying | An FPV whoop with goggles |
| **No-name AliExpress kits with generic radio** | Radios incompatible with the wider ecosystem; throwaway gear | RadioMaster + BNF drone |
| **"Drone in a box" gimmick kits** | Padded with cheap accessories; underlying drone is bad | Save the money for Stage 2 |
| **Premium first build** | You will crash; expensive parts hurt more | Budget build (see BUDGET-TIERS.md) |
| **5" without sim time first** | First flight = instant crash = damaged $400 of gear | Stages 0-2 first |
| **DJI Avata / Mini for "FPV"** | Camera drone with FPV mode; not a real FPV experience | A real whoop + ELRS radio |
